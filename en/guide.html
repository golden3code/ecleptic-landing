Lever 01  ·  Sleep
10minutes

Morning light sets your clock

Your circadian rhythm drives your energy, hunger, mood and hormones — and it gets set through your eyes. Natural morning light brings your evening melatonin forward: you fall asleep faster and sleep more deeply.

The protocol
  1. Within 60 minutes of waking up: 10 minutes outside, no sunglasses. A balcony or an open window counts.
  2. Overcast skies count too: they're still 10× brighter than a ceiling light.
Lever 02  ·  Energy
+90minutes

Delay your first coffee by 90 minutes

When you wake up, your cortisol is already at its peak: coffee adds almost nothing. Worse, caffeine blocks adenosine without clearing it — and it all comes crashing back down on you around 2 p.m.

The protocol
  1. First coffee 90 minutes after waking up, last one 8 to 10 hours before bed (half-life ≈ 5 h).
  2. Instead, when you wake up: a big glass of water. You lose ~500 ml (about 17 oz) a night just by breathing.
Lever 03  ·  Nutrition
2g / kg / day

Protein first, the rest follows

If you track only one nutrition number, make it this one: 1.6 to 2.2 g of protein per kilo of body weight (about 0.7 to 1 g per pound). The No. 1 factor in recovery and satiety — and therefore in weight control.

The protocol
  1. Aim for ~30 g per meal. Rule of thumb: a palm-sized portion of meat or fish ≈ 25–30 g.
  2. The meal people miss most often: breakfast. Eggs, skyr, cottage cheese.

That's exactly the math Ecleptic does for you: you snap a photo of your meal, the app counts the protein and adjusts your target for the day.

Lever 04  ·  Sleep
18°in the bedroom (64 °F)

Cool, dark, regular

Your body needs to drop its core temperature by ~1 °C (about 2 °F) to sink into deep sleep: a bedroom that's too warm is the No. 1 saboteur. And a regular sleep schedule matters more than how long you sleep.

The protocol
  1. Keep the bedroom between 17 and 19 °C (63–66 °F). A hot shower 1 to 2 hours before bed: it triggers the temperature drop that follows.
  2. A fixed bedtime, within ±30 minutes, weekends included. The hardest part — and the one that pays off most.
Lever 05  ·  Training
80%of your cardio

Zone 2, the engine everyone neglects

80% of endurance athletes' cardio is done at low intensity — and that's no accident. Zone 2 builds your mitochondria, your aerobic base and your VO₂max, the best measurable predictor of longevity.

The protocol
  1. 2 to 3 sessions of 30–45 minutes a week, at a pace where you can hold a conversation. Brisk uphill walking, cycling, rowing machine.
  2. The test: if you can't talk, slow down. Yes, it'll feel too easy. That's normal.
Lever 06  ·  Recovery
60seconds / morning

Listen to your data, not your motivation

Progress doesn't come from training, but from the recovery that follows it. Every morning, your resting heart rate and your HRV tell you whether your body has absorbed the day before. Pushing hard on a red day is digging yourself into a hole.

The protocol
  1. Every morning, 60 seconds: last night's sleep, resting heart rate, how you feel when you wake up.
  2. Red → light session or rest. Green → that's the day you push.

That's the heart of Ecleptic: a Readiness Score every morning, calculated from your real data (sleep, HRV, training load), that tells you whether today is a day to push or to recover.

Lever 07  ·  The meta-lever
14days

What gets measured improves

The real secret of “optimized” people is the feedback loop: measure → adjust → measure again. Without measurement, you're steering by feel — and feel lies (sleep deprivation impairs your judgment of your own state).

The protocol
  1. Pick 3 metrics max: hours of sleep, grams of protein, workouts per week. Log them for 14 days.
  2. Then look at what correlates with your best days, and adjust one lever at a time.
