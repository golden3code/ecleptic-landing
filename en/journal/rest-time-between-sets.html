How long should you rest between sets?

Strength and muscle: 2 to 3 minutes. Isolation: 1 to 2. Endurance: 30 to 60 s. Why resting too little cuts into your gains, and the one simple rule to follow.

By Auguste Phily-Priou, founder of Ecleptic

The short answer: for strength and muscle growth, rest 2 to 3 minutes between sets. On small isolation exercises, 1 to 2 minutes is enough. And if you're after muscular endurance or working through a circuit, 30 to 60 seconds. For a long time, people believed you had to keep rest short to keep the intensity up; recent science finds no advantage to short rest for building muscle — if anything, a small handicap.

The rule that sums it all up: rest long enough that your next set is still a good set. Muscle grows with quality volume — sets where you do your reps with a decent weight and clean technique. If you start again too soon, you're still out of breath and tired, you lose reps, and you mechanically reduce the useful work.

Why rest 2 to 3 minutes between sets to build muscle?

This is the counterintuitive point many people miss. It was long thought that short rest, by keeping the muscles under tension and driving up fatigue, promoted growth. More recent research shows the opposite:

  • Resting too little cuts the number of reps you can manage on the next set. Fewer quality reps means less effective volume, and so smaller gains. In a 2016 trial (21 trained lifters, 8 weeks), 3 minutes of rest produced more strength and more thigh muscle than 1 minute.
  • 2 to 3 minutes let your muscles and breathing recover enough to attack the next set with full strength. You keep your volume up throughout the session.
  • On big compound lifts (squat, bench press, pull-ups, deadlift), this is even more true: they work the whole body and demand more recovery between sets. According to a 2018 review (23 studies, 491 participants), trained lifters need more than 2 minutes to maximize strength; 1 to 2 minutes is enough for beginners.

In other words, cutting your rest short doesn't make your session more effective — it just makes it more tiring for a smaller result. The only real cost of 2 to 3 minutes is the extra time spent at the gym.

Keep the size of the effect in perspective: a 2024 meta-analysis (9 studies) finds only a small advantage to resting longer than 60 seconds for muscle growth, and no clear difference beyond 90 seconds; for strength, the American College of Sports Medicine (2026) sees no clear difference. Rest isn't magic: it just keeps you from sabotaging your volume.

How to manage your rest time without wasting it

  • Actually time it. About two minutes often ends up being 45 seconds when you're in a hurry, or 5 minutes when you're scrolling. A simple timer keeps your sessions honest.
  • Use your rest wisely: you can alternate two exercises for different muscle groups (a superset) to save time without shortening each muscle's rest.
  • Don't fill your rest with intense cardio if your goal is strength — you'd cut into the recovery your next set needs.

Rest between sets isn't dead time: it's what makes the next set productive. Managed well, it fits into a broader logic of progress — that of progressive overload, where every quality set counts toward adding, week after week, a little more weight or a few more reps.

Key takeaways

  • Strength and muscle growth: 2 to 3 minutes of rest, especially on big lifts — short rest cuts your useful volume.
  • Isolation: 1 to 2 minutes; endurance and circuits: 30 to 60 seconds.
  • The one rule: rest long enough that your next set is still a good set. Time it.

Frequently asked questions

How long should you rest between sets when lifting weights?

2 to 3 minutes for strength and muscle growth, especially on big lifts; 1 to 2 minutes for isolation exercises; 30 to 60 seconds for endurance or circuits. The rule: rest long enough that your next set stays high quality.

Does shorter rest build more muscle?

No, if anything it's the opposite. Resting too little cuts the number of reps you can manage on the next set, and with it your useful volume. Studies find a small advantage to resting longer than 60 seconds; on big lifts, aim for 2 to 3 minutes to keep your sets high quality.

How long should you rest between sets for strength?

At least 2 minutes on big lifts, and 3 to 5 minutes with very heavy loads (1 to 6 reps), according to the American College of Sports Medicine. Your nervous system needs to recover to produce force on every set. If you're a beginner, 1 to 2 minutes is enough.
