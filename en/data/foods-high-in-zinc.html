Foods high in zinc: ranked by France's official Ciqual table

According to the 2025 Ciqual table from ANSES, France's food safety agency, the top three everyday foods highest in zinc: “Beef, knuckle, boiled/cooked in water” (11 mg per 100 g), “Beef, braised” (10.5 mg) and “Sesame seed, grilled, husked” (10.2 mg).

By Auguste Phily-Priou, founder of Ecleptic

What it does

Zinc is an essential trace element: it is involved in the activity of about 300 enzymes and more than 2,500 transcription factors, the proteins that switch your genes on and off. A deficiency slows growth and weakens immune defenses. What makes zinc special is that absorption depends heavily on the rest of your plate. Phytates, found in grains and legumes, reduce it. That's why your needs shift with the way you eat, and why a single number on a label doesn't tell the whole story.

How much you need

The EU nutrient reference value (NRV) is 10 mg a day (Regulation 1169/2011). EFSA (2014) and France's ANSES go further: the reference depends on how much phytate you eat. For men, it ranges from 9.4 mg a day at 300 mg of phytate to 14 mg at 900 mg (16.3 mg at 1,200 mg according to EFSA); for women, from 7.5 to 11 mg (12.7 mg). The more whole grains and legumes you eat, the higher your need. Pregnancy and breastfeeding add 1.6 and 2.9 mg a day respectively, according to EFSA.

Practical tips

  • Meat and shellfish are the most direct sources. In the Ciqual table, braised beef provides 10.5 mg per 100 g (3.5 oz), cooked 5% fat ground beef 6.4 mg and canned crab 8.8 mg. ANSES also lists organ meats, cheese, legumes and fish.
  • Soak, sprout, ferment. Soaking legumes, sprouting seeds or fermenting (sourdough bread, for instance) lowers phytates and improves mineral availability, according to a review by Hotz and Gibson (2007).
  • Sesame, hemp and pumpkin seeds. They're the richest plant foods in the Ciqual table (7.8 to 10.2 mg per 100 g). Count them as a top-up, since portions are small.
  • Lots of whole grains and legumes? Then aim for the upper end of EFSA's range rather than the NRV, and talk to your doctor before taking any supplement.

How this ranking is built

Values per 100 g of the food as described (raw, cooked, drained…), as published by ANSES, France's food safety agency, in the 2025 Ciqual table (3484 foods; zinc content known for 2377 of them). The everyday ranking leaves out spices, herbs, seaweed and condiments, supplements and foods for special medical purposes, baby foods, alcohol, and raw meat or fish (the cooked version is kept). To keep the examples varied, only one food per main name is kept (the richest). Values listed as “traces” count as zero; values below the quantification limit are not ranked. % NRV compares the content per 100 g with the EU nutrient reference value (10 mg per day, Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011), the one used on food labels: a benchmark, not your personal requirement. One clearly atypical value was set aside: Tournesol, graine, grillé, salé : 36 mg/100 g, environ 7 fois les autres graines de tournesol de la table.

Frequently asked questions

Which food has the most zinc?

Top of the everyday foods in the 2025 Ciqual table from ANSES: “Beef, knuckle, boiled/cooked in water”, with 11 mg per 100 g, ahead of “Beef, braised” (10.5 mg) and “Sesame seed, grilled, husked” (10.2 mg).

How much zinc do I need per day?

According to EFSA, it depends on the phytate in your diet: from 9.4 to 16.3 mg a day for men and from 7.5 to 12.7 mg for women, the higher values applying to phytate-rich diets. The EU label NRV is 10 mg.

Which plant foods are high in zinc?

In the Ciqual table: roasted sesame seeds (10.2 mg per 100 g), hemp seeds (9.9 mg), pumpkin seeds (7.8 mg), dried lupin (4.75 mg) and cooked lentils (1.25 mg). Since phytates in grains and legumes reduce absorption, soaking, sprouting and fermenting help.
